Session-attributable text watermarking for autonomous agents provides verifiable provenance and accountability by embedding subtle, statistically detectable modifications into generated outputs during a specific user or system session. A primary benefit of this technique is its ability to deter misuse, track data leakage, and establish intellectual property rights without significantly degrading the fluency or utility of the natural language text produced by large language models. Furthermore, it enables developers and security teams to audit autonomous agent behaviors and trace malicious actions back to a compromised or rogue instance within a defined timeframe. However, significant limitations persist, including vulnerability to advanced cryptographic and semantic removal attacks, such as paraphrasing, synonym substitution, and translation, which can strip the watermark while preserving the underlying semantic meaning. Additionally, short sequences often lack sufficient entropy to embed robust watermarks reliably without introducing noticeable statistical anomalies or false positives. Consequently, while session-attributable text watermarking is a valuable layer in a comprehensive agent security posture, it cannot currently serve as a standalone guarantee of authenticity or integrity in adversarial environments.
