### Limitations

Despite rigorous cryptographic guarantees, several systemic limitations hinder provenance enforcement in autonomous AI ecosystems. First, deterministic verification fails to capture semantic drift or adversarial prompt injection that manifests entirely within valid syntactic structures; an agent may process a digitally signed, unaltered file containing malicious instructions that subvert its objective function. Second, key management at scale introduces severe operational friction. If an agent's private signing key is compromised via side-channel or memory-injection attacks, the entire downstream lineage becomes untrustworthy, necessitating complex revocation and pruning mechanisms. Finally, embedding comprehensive provenance metadata into high-frequency, low-latency tool outputs introduces substantial computational overhead, potentially degrading the real-time responsiveness required in multi-agent control loops.

### Evaluation Criteria

To rigorously assess the efficacy of secure provenance controls in autonomous multi-agent architectures, evaluations must measure several quantitative metrics. The primary criterion is lineage integrity verification latency, defined as the computational time required to cryptographically validate a dependency chain of length $N$. Second, resilience against forged provenance must be tested by injecting unsigned or maliciously re-signed tool outputs and measuring the system's detection and rejection rate. Third, operational throughput overhead must be benchmarked by comparing end-to-end task completion times with and without cryptographic signing enabled across file-sharing events. Finally, false positive rates regarding benign execution rejections due to strict key revocation policies must be evaluated to ensure system availability.
